\section*{Abstract}

{\small
The emergence of AI-RAN architectures for future 6G networks envisions deep integration
between radio access network (RAN) infrastructure and AI workloads. While prior work
primarily focuses on AI-for-RAN and AI-on-RAN, the potential of AI-and-RAN, sharing RAN
compute infrastructure with non-RAN (AI) workloads, remains largely unexplored. In this
paper, we investigate the feasibility of leveraging GPU-accelerated RAN infrastructure to
support opportunistic training of foundation models (FMs), one of the most compute-intensive
AI workloads. We conduct the first comprehensive characterization of spare GPU capacity in
GPU-accelerated RAN systems at both micro-scale (per-slot within a cell site) and macro-scale
(across sites). Our analysis reveals substantial underutilized compute resources, approximately
40--85\%, that are temporally bursty within sites yet spatially complementary across sites. To
efficiently exploit this opportunity in a RAN-safe manner, we present \textbf{Weaver}, a system
that enables FM training to opportunistically share GPU resources with latency-critical RAN
workloads while preserving RAN performance. Weaver adopts a RAN-first design with coordinated
elasticity across system layers. It introduces a RAN-centric spare-compute controller embedded
in the MAC scheduler to stabilize spare GPU capacity, alongside a two-level elastic FM training
framework that adapts to dynamic resources across and within sites. Evaluations on an
O-RAN-aligned 5G NR prototype and multi-site testbed show that Weaver reduces spare-compute
fluctuations by up to 4.9$\times$ and achieves 2.1--4.2$\times$ higher training throughput than
baseline approaches while harvesting up to 83\% of available spare compute.
}

\section*{My contribution}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item\small{\textbf{Characterisation and profiling.} Ran the GPU profiling and roofline analysis
    behind the spare-capacity characterisation, showing that RAN kernels are memory-bandwidth-bound
    while FM training is compute-bound, the asymmetry that makes SM-level co-location viable.
    \vspace{-3pt}}
  \item\small{\textbf{Controller evaluation.} Evaluated the RAN-centric spare-compute controller,
    including instrumenting the MAC scheduler to measure per-deadline completion and confirm that
    live RAN performance is preserved while capacity is harvested. \vspace{-3pt}}
  \item\small{\textbf{Large-scale experiments.} Carried out the large-scale distributed FM training
    runs on the multi-site testbed used in the evaluation. \vspace{-3pt}}
\end{itemize}
