\section{GPU and Accelerator Toolchain}
  \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \tool{CUDA (runtime and driver API)}{primary}
      {Pinned host memory (\texttt{cudaHostAlloc}, \texttt{cudaHostRegister}) with a bucketed pool of my own design; stream and event based timing; launching PTX/cubin kernels directly through the driver API in my MLIR compiler project.}
    \tool{GPU sharing mechanisms}{working}
      {SM-level partitioning via CUDA green contexts and MPS, used to co-locate a training workload with a latency-critical tenant on one device; measured the reconfiguration overheads that make one mechanism preferable to the other.}
    \tool{Profiling and performance analysis}{working}
      {Nsight-based kernel profiling, arithmetic-intensity and roofline analysis to establish whether a kernel is compute- or memory-bandwidth-bound --- the analysis underpinning the co-location argument in \textit{Weaver}. Also SM occupancy, memory-throughput and interference characterisation between co-resident workloads.}
    \tool{cuBLAS / cuBLASXt}{working}
      {GEMM execution paths in the Morphling worker pool, including multi-GPU dispatch and correctness checking against a CPU (MKL) reference.}
    \tool{PyTorch}{working}
      {Distributed foundation-model training runs at multi-site scale; intercepting backend dispatch and hooking autograd for emulation and scheduling purposes.}
  \end{itemize}

\section{Networking and Data-Plane Toolchain}
  \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \tool{libevent / event-driven asynchronous I/O}{primary}
      {The Morphling transport is built on it: non-blocking connection handling, event-loop threads, buffered reads and writes, zero-copy scatter-gather via \texttt{evbuffer\_add\_reference} with shared-pointer cleanup. I also diagnosed and fixed connection-cleanup and device-failure deadlocks that only appeared at scale.}
    \tool{Serialisation frameworks}{working}
      {Protocol Buffers and FlatBuffers, benchmarked against each other along the full send path (serialise / size / transmit) to choose the framing and batching strategy; wrote the analysis up as design documents in the repository.}
    \tool{Measurement and diagnosis}{working}
      {iperf3 throughput comparison against real hardware, per-slot completion-time distributions, packet and timeline tracing, socket-state diagnosis (\texttt{netstat}, connection lifecycle) --- used both to validate emulator fidelity and to find production-style bugs.}
    \tool{5G RAN stacks}{working}
      {OpenAirInterface and Open5GS: building and running a live 5G stack, instrumenting the MAC scheduler for deadline measurement, and driving traffic through it for experiments.}
    \tool{RDMA / SmartNIC / programmable switches}{familiar --- to learn}
      {I have not yet worked with RDMA verbs, SmartNICs or P4. I state this plainly: my data-plane experience is at the host software layer, and moving it down into the network is precisely what attracts me to iSING Lab.}
  \end{itemize}

\section{Compiler Toolchain}
  \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \tool{LLVM}{primary}
      {IR-level pass development for \textit{CoSenseAQ} (first-author manuscript): variable-range analysis, type shrinkage and automatic quantization driven by sensor specifications, evaluated on embedded ARM targets for power, performance and area.}
    \tool{MLIR}{working}
      {Sole author of \textit{TensorOps}, a multi-target tensor compiler: a custom dialect, operator fusion (matmul+relu) at the dialect level, lowering through Linalg to LLVM for CPU JIT and to NVVM/ROCDL for GPU, ending in PTX/cubin launched via the CUDA Driver API.}
    \tool{Cross-compilation and embedded toolchains}{working}
      {ARM Cortex-M0 and Cortex-A53 toolchains, binary size and cycle-level measurement on real hardware.}
  \end{itemize}
