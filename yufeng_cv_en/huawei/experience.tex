      {Flux-DT --- Elastic, Preemption-Tolerant Runtime for Stateful Workloads on Spare Compute}{\textbf{MSc by Research Thesis}}
      {University of Edinburgh --- sole author}{Sep 2025 -- Present}
      \resumeItemListStart
        \resumeItem{State that must survive preemption}
          {Existing compute-sharing designs assume the harvesting workload is stateless and disposable. Built a runtime for a workload that is neither: it holds scene state that must survive interruption, so reclaiming its compute cannot simply mean killing it. The runtime lets such a workload run on bursty, reclaimable capacity without violating the priority tenant's guarantees or corrupting its own state.}
        \resumeItem{Near-real-time control policy}
          {Designed and implemented a control loop that answers resource pressure with a graded set of actions --- relocation, fidelity reduction, suspend and resume --- rather than a single eviction decision, on the timescale the priority workload imposes.}
        \resumeItem{Scheduling heuristic and shedding order}
          {Formulated work placement as a joint objective across interfering units and \textbf{designed a greedy heuristic} to decide which work is worth doing; the same ranking determines the shedding order when compute is reclaimed mid-solve.}
      \resumeItemListEnd
